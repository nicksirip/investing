## Understanding the Post-WW2 Reset Spectrum

A post-war financial reset unfolds in identifiable stages. Governments use policy — fiscal spending, financial repression, and inflation tolerance — to manage down elevated debt burdens over many years while anchoring a new productivity expansion. Positioning must account for the current stage of that cycle, not just short-term macro signals.

| Level | Description | Example Conditions |
|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| **1 – Pre-Reset / Normal Expansion** | Low debt stress; normal monetary policy; no structural reset underway | Debt/GDP <80%, CPI 2%, real yields positive, equities at fair value |
| **2 – Reset Signals Emerging** | Debt/GDP rising; governments resisting deep austerity; inflation tolerance widening | Debt/GDP 80–120%, CPI 3–4%, real yields near zero, fiscal deficits persistent |
| **3 – Active Financial Repression** | Policy holds rates below inflation; debt managed via nominal growth; rebuilding capex rising | Real rates negative, CPI 4–6%, industrial capex supercycle beginning, infrastructure spending surging |
| **4 – Full Rebuilding Cycle** | Multi-year productivity expansion; inflation normalized at 3–4%; technology and energy leadership emerging | Sustained real GDP growth 2–3%+, capex above trend, corporate earnings broad-based, value/cyclical outperformance |
| **5 – Reset Completion / Transition** | Debt burden reduced; real rates normalize; long-run equilibrium re-established — or policy misstep triggers disorderly exit | Debt/GDP declining, CPI settling toward 2%, central bank normalization; risk of stagflation if policy missteps occur |

## Key Risk Factors to Monitor

### Debt & Fiscal Indicators
- Government debt-to-GDP ratios (IMF Fiscal Monitor, BIS) — rising trajectories signal prolonged repression
- Fiscal deficit trajectory — persistent deficits confirm the government growth + inflation policy preference
- Sovereign credit rating actions — downgrades signal market loss of confidence in fiscal management
- Primary balance (deficit excluding interest) — improvement signals sustainability; deterioration signals monetization risk

### Inflation & Real Yield Environment
- CPI, Core CPI, and PCE deflator — watch for sustained tolerance of above-target inflation
- TIPS breakeven rates (5Y and 10Y) — market-implied inflation compensation; rising breakevens confirm repression regime
- Real 10-year yield (nominal 10Y minus TIPS 10Y) — negative or near-zero real yields are the defining condition of financial repression
- Fed and ECB policy rate vs. CPI differential — deeply negative policy real rates confirm the regime

### Industrial & Capex Cycle Indicators
- ISM Manufacturing PMI and new orders component — sustained above-50 expansion in manufacturing confirms rebuilding cycle
- US Census Bureau Capital Goods Orders (non-defense, ex-aircraft) — proxy for private business investment in hard assets
- Infrastructure spending legislation and execution (IIJA, CHIPS Act, equivalent EU programs) — confirms structural capex
- Semiconductor and energy capex announcements — technology and energy leadership investment cycle proxy

### Labor & Productivity Indicators
- Nonfarm productivity (BLS quarterly) — rising trend confirms productivity expansion thesis; stagnation signals risk
- Unit labor cost growth — labor cost inflation above productivity growth erodes the real wage component of the reset
- Labor force participation rate — recovering participation supports the long-run growth and tax-base thesis
- Manufacturing and construction job creation (payrolls breakdown) — confirms domestic rebuilding and reshoring trend

## Hedging Strategies

### Inflation Hedges

1. **TIPS (TIP, VTIP, STIP)** – Inflation-adjusted principal; core fixed-income instrument in a repression regime; VTIP for shorter duration, TIP for intermediate; hold in tax-deferred accounts to avoid phantom income taxation
2. **I-Bonds (TreasuryDirect)** – Guaranteed CPI-linked inflation protection; $10,000/year per taxpayer limit; federal-tax-deferred until redemption; ideal complement to TIPS in taxable accounts
3. **Gold (GLD, IAU)** – Non-yielding hard asset; historically strong when real rates are negative or near zero, which is the defining feature of financial repression; hold in IRA/Roth for tax efficiency (gold ETFs taxed as collectibles at 28% maximum rate)
4. **Commodity basket (DBC, PDBC)** – Broad commodity exposure; real-asset diversification that appreciates with inflation; roll methodology reduces contango drag; Section 1256 blended tax rate (60% long-term / 40% short-term)

### Financial Repression Hedges

1. **Reduce nominal long-duration bonds** – Shift bond allocation from TLT/BLV toward TIP/VTIP to protect real purchasing power; avoid locking in below-inflation yields for long durations
2. **Overweight real-asset equities (infrastructure, energy, materials)** – Companies with inflation pass-through revenue naturally hedge financial repression without the tax complexity of commodity ETFs
3. **Floating-rate instruments (BKLN)** – Senior secured floating-rate loans reprice upward as short rates rise; provides income hedge if policy normalization begins unexpectedly

### Interest Rate Transition Hedges

1. **Short-duration bond ladder** – Hold intermediate bonds (2–7 year duration); reinvest at higher yields as rates normalize; avoid concentration in long-end that is most exposed to repricing
2. **Short TLT puts or TBF (inverse TLT)** – If real rates begin to normalize aggressively, long-duration nominal bonds face outsized losses; defined-risk hedge via TLT puts
3. **REIT selectivity** – Prefer global infrastructure REITs (AMT, PLD, BIPC) with inflation escalators over domestic leveraged REITs that face cap-rate expansion from rising real rates

### Equity Volatility Hedges

- **SPY put spreads** – Defined-risk protection against equity drawdowns during policy transitions (rate normalization, fiscal shock)
- **Long VIX calls (60–90 day tenor)** – Policy uncertainty and debt ceiling events create volatility spikes; limited-cost insurance during known risk windows
- **Sector rotation within equities** – Shift from speculative growth (QQQ) to value/quality (IVE, QUAL) as rates rise; reduces beta without exiting equities entirely

### Tax-Efficiency Considerations

- **TIPS in tax-advantaged accounts (IRA/401k)** — Inflation accrual on principal is taxed as ordinary income annually even without cash distribution; holding TIPS in tax-deferred accounts defers this phantom income tax
- **Gold ETFs (GLD, IAU) in IRA/Roth IRA** — Gold ETFs are classified as collectibles (28% maximum long-term capital gains rate); holding inside tax-advantaged accounts eliminates the collectible rate penalty
- **Infrastructure MLP vs. C-corp structures** — C-corp infrastructure companies (KMI, WMB, BIPC) avoid K-1 complexity and unrelated business taxable income (UBTI) in IRAs; preferable for tax simplicity
- **Commodity ETFs (DBC) in tax-advantaged accounts** — Section 1256 blended rate applies regardless of holding period; minimize in taxable accounts

## Scenario-Based Positioning

### Scenario A: Reset Signals Emerging (Level 2)

**Probability driver:** Debt/GDP above 100% and rising, government resisting austerity, CPI running 3–4%, real yields near zero, early-stage infrastructure spending commitments

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Begin shifting bond allocation from TLT to TIP/VTIP | Replace nominal long-duration with inflation-linked; real yields near zero make nominal bonds unattractive |
| Add XLE, XME, IFRA 5% | Begin capex cycle and real-asset exposure at early stage |
| Add GLD 3–5% | Early inflation and repression hedge; attractive at low real yields |
| Tilt equity toward value factor (IVE, IWVL) | Value outperforms growth in rising-inflation, early-rate environments |
| Maintain broad equity core (VT, VIG) | Early-stage; stay equity-heavy; do not over-hedge |
| Add domestic small/mid-cap (IWM, IJH) 5% | Begin local rebuilding exposure |

### Scenario B: Active Financial Repression (Level 3)

**Probability driver:** Real policy rates clearly negative, CPI 4–6%, infrastructure capex programs executing, industrial reshoring announcements accelerating, TIPS breakevens rising above 3%

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Increase TIP/VTIP to 8–10% of total portfolio | Core inflation protection; negative real rates make TIPS compelling vs. nominal bonds |
| Max I-Bond purchases ($10K/taxpayer) | Tax-deferred federal inflation protection; no state/local tax |
| Increase XLE, XME, IFRA, BIPC to 12–15% combined | Full real-asset and capex cycle exposure |
| Increase domestic small/mid-cap (IWM, IJH) to 12–15% | Reshoring and local rebuilding at full intensity |
| Reduce TLT/BLV; eliminate long-duration nominal bonds | Negative real yields make long-duration nominal bonds capital destruction in real terms |
| Add commodities (DBC) 5% | Broad real-asset diversification; input demand from rebuilding cycle |
| Maintain equity core tilted to value/quality (IVE, QUAL, SCHD) | Stay equity-heavy; shift away from speculative growth |

### Scenario C: Full Rebuilding Cycle (Level 4)

**Probability driver:** Sustained real GDP growth 2–3%+, broad corporate earnings expansion, capex above trend, technology leadership emerging, debt/GDP declining gradually, inflation normalized at 3–4%

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Hold maximum equity weight (65–70%) | Long productivity expansion; stay equity-heavy; compounding is the primary driver |
| Maintain real-asset sleeve (REITs, infrastructure, commodities/gold) 15% | Real assets continue to hedge residual inflation; infrastructure provides stable income |
| Broaden global equity exposure (VT, IWVL) | Global rebuilding supports international and EM recovery; diversify across geographies |
| Begin gradually extending bond duration selectively | As inflation stabilizes and real yields normalize, some duration extension becomes appropriate |
| Trim speculative growth if rates rise sharply | Reduce QQQ/high-multiple names if real rates normalize unexpectedly fast |
| Rebalance semiannually; DCA monthly | Stick to discipline; prevent drift from large equity gains |

### Scenario D: Disorderly Reset / Policy Misstep (Level 5 risk)

**Probability driver:** Inflation accelerating above 6–7% without policy response (stagflation risk), or sharp policy overtightening triggering deep recession, or sovereign credit stress emerging

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Increase inflation hedges (GLD, DBC, TIP) to 15–20% | Disorderly inflation requires maximum hard-asset protection |
| Reduce equity exposure 10–15%; shift to quality/staples (QUAL, XLP) | Earnings compression risk in stagflation scenario; quality and pricing power preserve capital |
| Short TLT via puts if inflation is disorderly | Long-duration nominal bonds devastated by unanchored inflation |
| Increase cash reserve to 8–10% | Optionality for re-entry after policy response stabilizes markets |
| Consult annual regime review; consider full repositioning | Policy misstep may require full portfolio restructure toward stagflation or recession guide |

## Key Indicators & Triggers

### Early Warning Signals (Begin Reset Positioning)
- Government debt-to-GDP above 100% and rising for two or more consecutive fiscal years
- Real 10-year yield (nominal minus TIPS breakeven) declining toward or below zero
- TIPS 5Y and 10Y breakeven rates rising above 3% and sustained
- Fiscal deficit persistently above 5% of GDP without credible consolidation plan
- Infrastructure or industrial policy spending programs announced or passing into law
- Reshoring and capex announcements accelerating in manufacturing, semiconductors, energy

### Active Repression Confirmation Signals (Full Rebuilding Positioning)
- CPI running 3–6% with policy rates held below or near inflation (real rates negative or near zero)
- ISM Manufacturing PMI above 52 for three or more consecutive months with new orders component rising
- Capital goods orders (non-defense, ex-aircraft) rising more than 10% year-over-year
- Infrastructure project groundbreakings and spend execution running on or ahead of legislative schedule
- Labor market tightening in construction, manufacturing, and energy (wage growth in these sectors above 4%)
- Global commodity prices broadly elevated on structural demand (copper, steel, aggregates, energy)

### Reset Completion / Normalization Signals (Review Inflation Hedges)
- Real 10-year yield rising back to +1% or above (repression regime ending)
- CPI declining toward 2% target for three or more consecutive months
- Debt/GDP declining for two or more consecutive years
- Fed or ECB signaling a sustained tightening path (not just tactical adjustments)
- TIPS breakeven rates declining toward 2–2.5%

### Policy Misstep / Disorderly Exit Signals (Switch to Stagflation or Recession Guide)
- CPI accelerating above 6% without policy response — consult stagflation guide
- Sharp policy overtightening causing recession signals — consult US or global recession guide
- Sovereign credit rating downgrade or Treasury market dysfunction — consult US debt crisis guide
