## Core Thesis

Use a strategy designed for a long rebuilding cycle where public debt is high and policymakers prefer growth plus manageable inflation over deep deleveraging.

- Favor productive assets over idle cash: equities, infrastructure, and real assets.
- Assume ongoing policy influence (fiscal and monetary) that can suppress real returns on cash and long-duration nominal bonds.
- Prioritize regions and sectors linked to industrial renewal, energy security, and technology leadership.

---

## Portfolio Structure (Long-Term Base Case)

| Sleeve | Target Weight | Objective |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Global equities | 50% | Capture broad productivity and earnings growth over a long cycle |
| Domestic small/mid-cap equities | 15% | Benefit from local capex, reindustrialization, and supply-chain localization |
| Real assets | 15% | Hedge inflation and own hard-asset cash flow (REITs, infrastructure, commodities/gold) |
| Intermediate high-quality bonds | 15% | Stabilize drawdowns without excessive long-duration risk; include inflation-linked bonds |
| Cash reserve | 5% | Opportunistic deployment during dislocations |

### Suggested Sub-Sleeve Mixes

**Real Assets (15%)**
- 6% infrastructure
- 5% REITs
- 4% commodities/gold

**Intermediate Bonds (15%)**
- 9% intermediate nominal high-quality bonds
- 6% inflation-linked bonds (TIPS or equivalent)

---

## Portfolio Rules

1. **Rebalance semiannually** to keep risk aligned with targets.
2. **Contribute monthly (DCA)** instead of deploying all capital at once.
3. **Cap single-position exposure** (example: 5% maximum per holding).
4. **Increase inflation hedges** when inflation regime risk rises (higher breakevens, persistent CPI acceleration).
5. **Reduce speculative growth exposure** when real rates rise sharply or policy turns restrictive.

## How This Mirrors Post-WW2 Dynamics

| Historical Dynamic | Portfolio Interpretation |
|--------------------|--------------------------|
| Multi-year productivity expansion and reconstruction | Maintain an equity-heavy core to compound with growth |
| Debt management through policy support and mild inflation | Avoid overconcentration in long-duration nominal bonds and cash |
| Structural rebuilding of industry and infrastructure | Own cyclical/value equities and real assets tied to physical investment |

---

## Risk Controls

- Keep **6–12 months of living expenses** outside the investment portfolio.
- Prefer **broad, diversified funds/ETFs** to reduce single-company risk.
- Run an **annual regime review** for deep recession, deflation shock, policy reversal, or structural credit stress.
- Maintain tax-aware account location for inflation-linked assets and higher-turnover sleeves.

---

## Implementation Variants

The base case above is a balanced profile. You can tilt this framework based on risk tolerance:

- **Conservative:** Increase bonds/cash, reduce small/mid and commodities.
- **Balanced:** Use the base allocation in this guide.
- **Aggressive:** Increase equities/real assets, reduce bond ballast.

---

## Review Triggers

Reassess allocations if one or more of the following occurs:

- Persistent disinflation/deflation that reduces need for inflation hedges
- Sharp policy tightening that materially increases recession risk
- Credit stress in sovereign or private markets
- Major geopolitical de-escalation that lowers commodity and defense risk premia
- Structural productivity surprise (up or down) that changes long-run return expectations
